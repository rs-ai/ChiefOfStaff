# Stakeholder Communication Guide

## Strategy Per Quadrant

### Manage Closely (High Power, High Interest)
- These are your make-or-break stakeholders
- Weekly or biweekly 1:1 touchpoints
- No surprises — ever. Brief them before announcements
- Seek their input early so they feel ownership
- Understand their personal success metrics

### Keep Satisfied (High Power, Low Interest)
- They can block you but won't pay attention until something goes wrong
- Monthly executive summaries — one page max
- Lead with business outcomes, not technical details
- Escalate risks early so they hear it from you first
- Make their sign-off easy: clear asks, pre-aligned with their deputies

### Keep Informed (Low Power, High Interest)
- Often mid-level managers or technical leads affected by your work
- Regular updates via existing channels (Slack, email, town halls)
- Leverage them as champions and feedback sources
- They can become powerful allies or vocal critics — invest accordingly

### Monitor (Low Power, Low Interest)
- Minimal effort — periodic updates via broadcast comms
- Watch for shifts: a reorg can move someone into a power quadrant overnight

## What Each C-Suite Role Cares About

| Role | Primary Concern | They Want to Hear | Common Pitfalls |
|------|----------------|-------------------|------------------------|
| **CEO** | Growth, strategy, competitive position | "This gives us a market advantage" | Leading with technical details instead of business outcomes |
| **CFO** | ROI, cost control, risk | "Here's the payback period and TCO" | Requesting budget without clear ROI justification |
| **CIO/CTO** | Architecture, security, scalability | "This fits our tech strategy and standards" | Building outside established platforms without alignment |
| **COO** | Efficiency, reliability, execution | "This reduces cycle time by X%" | Presenting unvalidated results as production-ready |
| **CHRO** | Talent, culture, workforce impact | "This upskills our team" | Framing automation without addressing workforce development |
| **Chief Legal/Risk** | Compliance, liability, governance | "Here's our risk mitigation plan" | Deferring compliance considerations to later phases |

## Meeting Prep Framework

Before every executive meeting, answer these 5 questions:

1. **What does this person care about most right now?**
   Not in general — right now. Check recent all-hands, earnings calls, their OKRs.

2. **What is my one ask or one key message?**
   If you have five points, you have zero. Pick one.

3. **What question will they ask that I'm not ready for?**
   Prepare for the hard question. If you can't answer it, say so honestly and commit to a follow-up.

4. **What's in it for them specifically?**
   Frame everything through their goals, not yours.

5. **What's the next action I want them to take?**
   Approve, fund, sponsor, unblock, connect — be specific.

## Influence Tactics by Situation

| Situation | Tactic |
|-----------|--------|
| Need budget approval | Lead with ROI and risk of inaction, not features |
| Facing resistance to change | Find one credible internal champion, pilot with their team first |
| Competing with another initiative | Don't attack — show complementarity or clear differentiation |
| Lost your sponsor | Move fast — identify a new one within 2 weeks or pause the initiative |
| Need cross-functional alignment | Pre-align 1:1 before the group meeting. Never surprise people in a room |
